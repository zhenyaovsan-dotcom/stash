# FOMO & attention liquidity

Memecoin prices often react to attention as much as to conventional financial information. Attention can arrive through a meme, an account, a community, a trend, or a visible cluster of traders. It can also disappear quickly.

## Attention is uneven

A token can receive a burst of posts, replies and watchlist additions in a very short window. The same signal may reach different traders at different times, and public activity does not show how much capital is actually available to buy. Views, likes, mentions and holder counts each measure something different.

“Attention liquidity” is a useful metaphor, not an on-chain metric: it describes how easily a token can attract enough active interest for trades to happen. Do not confuse social activity with pool depth.

## How FOMO distorts the read

- **Urgency:** “right now or never” framing discourages checking the mint and route.
- **Social proof:** seeing other wallets buy can make a move feel safer without changing the underlying risk.
- **Anchoring:** traders may use a recent high as the price a token “should” revisit.
- **Loss chasing:** a missed trade or loss can trigger a lower-quality entry.
- **Survivorship bias:** winning screenshots travel further than failed transactions and abandoned wallets.

## A small pause that helps

Before acting, write one sentence for the reason to enter and one condition that would prove that reason wrong. If the only reason is “everyone is buying”, identify what evidence shows actual demand, what the exit liquidity looks like, and what happens if the next set of buyers never arrives.

## What to observe carefully

Social signals can help explain why a token attracts interest, but they are noisy and easy to manipulate. Use them alongside independent evidence: actual swaps, reserve changes, wallet activity and the ability to exit at a realistic size.
