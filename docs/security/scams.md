# Common memecoin scams

Memecoin risks include ordinary market risk, misleading promotion, deceptive interfaces and transactions designed to move assets without the user understanding what they signed. A polished website or active social account does not remove these risks.

## Fake links and impersonation

Scammers copy token names, avatars, account handles and website layouts. Confirm the mint address from more than one trustworthy channel. A ticker is not unique, and a search result or reply can lead to an imitation site.

## Malicious transaction prompts

A wallet prompt can request transfers, approvals, authority changes, or interactions with an unfamiliar program. Read the action and destination shown by your wallet before signing. If the wallet or site cannot explain the effect in understandable terms, do not proceed.

Never enter a seed phrase or private key into a website, chat, support form or browser extension. A legitimate service does not need your recovery phrase to show a transaction.

## Liquidity and distribution risks

A token may be technically tradable while still having shallow liquidity, highly concentrated holders, or large wallets able to sell into the market. “Rug pull” is often used broadly; analyze the specific evidence—such as liquidity removal, large unexplained transfers, misleading claims or creator-linked selling—rather than treating every price collapse as the same event.

## A safer pre-sign routine

- Verify the network and exact mint address.
- Navigate to known official domains rather than links in unsolicited replies.
- Inspect the transaction recipient, program and expected effect.
- Keep a separate wallet for experiments and limit its balance.
- Reject unexpected signatures or requests for seed phrases.
- Use reputable explorers and security tools as independent checks, not as guarantees.
- Revoke permissions only through a trusted interface and understand the transaction before signing.

## Red flags that require more checking

- Promises of guaranteed profit or pressure to act immediately.
- A creator or “support” account asking for a recovery phrase.
- A transaction unrelated to the action you intended.
- Fake audit badges or unverifiable ownership claims.
- Inconsistent mint addresses across official-looking posts.
- A dashboard claim about locked liquidity or renounced authority with no direct evidence.

## What a security tool cannot prove

Risk scanners use heuristics and available metadata. They can miss new attack patterns, misclassify unusual but legitimate behavior, or show a low-risk label despite market and liquidity risks. A clean scan is not a guarantee of safety.

## If you suspect a compromise

Stop signing transactions. Do not pay a stranger who promises to recover funds. From a clean device, review recent wallet activity, move remaining assets to a new wallet whose seed phrase has never been exposed, and seek help from official wallet support. Recovery is not guaranteed; do not reveal sensitive credentials while asking for help.
